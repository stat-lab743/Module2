Same sample mean. Different sample sizes.

Complete three short parts, targeting 20–25 minutes. Submit one table with SE, z, and upper-tail probabilities, your calculations, and three short written responses in Canvas. This webpage checks calculations but does not save or submit your answers.

A psychology research team is evaluating average attention scores in three hypothetical studies. Each randomized scenario provides a reference population mean and known standard deviation. All three studies share the same sample mean, but have increasing sample sizes. Investigate how unusual each result would be under the reference population.

Assume independent random observations from a normal population. This lets us use normal sampling distributions even for Study A’s small sample. The sample mean M summarizes a group; it is not an individual participant’s score.

This table gives the area to the right: P(Z ≥ z). All generated study means are above μ, so use the positive z value directly. If your textbook gives area to the left, subtract it from 1. If it gives area between the mean and z, subtract it from 0.5000.

Example: for z = 0.65, use row 0.6 and column .05. The upper-tail probability is 0.2578, or 25.78%. In this lab, the probability refers to a sample mean at least as high as the observed M, assuming the reference population—not the probability that the population mean is correct.
